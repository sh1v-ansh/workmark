A — gold on the top tier only

Gold #D9A93C on ink #4A3106 — violet #6142F5 keeps every action.

Scarcity is what makes it a reward. Six of thirty-seven skills are gold, so gold means something a student worked for rather than something the page is wearing. Colour the whole ramp and the top tier stops being distinguishable from the bottom one — which is exactly the failure the current card has.

The gradient is what keeps it from looking cheap. Flat yellow reads as a highlighter; a light-to-deep sweep reads as metal. That difference is the whole distance between a medal and a novelty sticker, and it costs one line of CSS.

It doesn’t collide with anything. Your caution state is dark brown text on pale cream inside an alert box. This is dark ink on a saturated metal fill inside a chip. Nobody confuses the two, and gold sits nowhere near violet, green or blue on the wheel — so the page finally has warm and cool in it.

If it still feels like too much, B is the restrained version: one seal per card, chips left alone. Take it if you ever want gold somewhere else too.
